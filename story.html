Our story

A restaurant built around a fire.

In an Armenian house, the ojakh is the hearth the family gathers around.

The tonir came first. It was built before the walls were finished, lined with clay from Aragatsotn, and lit for three days before anything was baked in it.

The kitchen cooks the food we grew up with and changes as little as it can: lavash pressed onto the oven wall by hand, meat over apricot wood, herbs from the market each morning.

What is modern here is the room, the pace and the plates. The recipes are older than all of us.
